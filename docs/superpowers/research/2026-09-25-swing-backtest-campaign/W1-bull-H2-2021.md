Scripts and intermediate data are in `swing_bt/w1/`.

**Alignment caveat (applies throughout).** `pv(D)` is stamped at 12:00 on D and carries the close of D−1. The engine's +6.23% therefore
runs from the 07-09 close (all cash) to the **12-30 close**. `metrics.json` compares that with SPY from the 07-12 close to the 12-31 close
(+9.40%), which is off by one session. Like for like, SPY-TR is **+10.07%** (07-09c→12-30c), or +10.09% from the 07-12 open, so the real
gap is **−3.84 pp**, not −3.17. Also, `trade_steps 171` in `metrics.json` counts each fill twice (final and non-final rows); there are **85 fills**.

## 3. Regime

- **122 sessions: 115 ACTIVE, 7 BLOCKED, 0 bear-mode days.** The blocked-days counter peaked at 5 (12-07) and reset on the ACTIVE 11-30 session, so it never came near 10.
- **All 7 blocks came from VIX** (prior-close readings 25.7, 28.6, 27.2, 31.1, 27.9, 30.7, 27.2):
  09-21, 11-29, and 12-01 through 12-07. The SPY > SMA200 × 1.03 leg never fired. Its closest call was **10-05, SPY only +0.72% above the threshold**,
  at the local low before the +7% October rally, so the gate nearly blocked entries at the worst possible moment.
- **Effect on this run:**
  - 09-21 and 11-29: 8/8 slots were full, so there was no effect. (FANG's target hit intraday on 11-29 and GOOG entered on 11-30 anyway.)
  - 12-01/12-02: 1 slot idle while SPY moved −0.5%.
  - **12-03 → 12-07: 3 slots idle (cash 36%) while SPY rose +2.1% (12-03 open → 12-08 open).** The gate lifted only on 12-08, once the
    rebound was done (VIX 21.9 on 12-07).
  - Net, the gate **hurt by about 0.7 pp** (SPY-equivalent exposure on the idle slots) and saved nothing.
- **Against the earlier research:** "the drawdown benefit comes from the regime filter" cannot be tested in this window. The filter barely
  engaged, and the strategy's drawdown (−5.98%) was deeper than SPY's (−5.11%). It happened while SPY was flat, so it came from stock picks, not from the market.

## 4. Funnel

The typical ACTIVE session, as mean (median):

universe 507 → not held 499.5 → has indicators 493.6 (493) → close > SMA200 **368.6 (363)** → RSI < 50 and rising **44.9 (32)** →
MACD histogram up 34.2 (21) → volume > 20-day average **11.8 (7)** → ADX > 15 10.1 (6) → rr_ok 10.1 → **PASSED 10.1 (6)**.

- **RSI < 50 and rising is the killer.** It removes 324 of about 369 trend-qualified names (keeps 12%). SMA200 removes 125 and volume 22.5
  (keeps 36%). ADX removes 1.7 and is nearly non-binding. rr_ok is a constant and never binds.
- Average PASSED by month: Jul 18.7, Aug 6.3, Sep 14.4, Oct 12.1, Nov 4.4, Dec 6.5. Three sessions had 0 passers; 34 had 3 or fewer.
- **Slots.** 78 of 115 sessions (68%) started with 8/8 held. But from 09-01 to 11-26 the 8th "position" was a $1.5–2k stub (see §6),
  and 45 of the 80 closes with 8 names held still carried 11–14% cash. The book was genuinely full on only about 35 closes.
- **Which passers entered was decided by list order, not signal.** Candidates are visited in today's S&P list order, which is alphabetical
  by company name: Aflac, Altria, AIG, Ameriprise, ADM… On 07-12, 50 names passed and the first eight distinct-sector names A–F were
  bought. The other 42 were skipped: 12 for no slot and 30 for sector (10 itemised, 20 in the summary line).
- **Sector cap.** It fired 807 times (522 itemised plus 285 in the "+N more" summaries), against 309 no-slot skips. It left slots empty on
  only 8 sessions: 10-18, 10-27, 10-28, 10-29, 11-19, 11-22 (3 slots), 12-21 and 12-31. That is 10 idle slot-sessions, a small cost.
- **Did capped names later outperform? No.** I bracket-simulated each name: −6%/+9% on the prior close, entry at the next open, up to 40 days, using yfinance OHLC.

  | Group | Unique names | 20-day excess vs RSP | Bracket-sim excess vs RSP | Share stopped |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Entered | 43 | +1.02% (s.e. 1.05) | +1.19% | 44% |
  | Sector-capped | 381 | −0.91% (s.e. 0.38) | −0.64% | 49% |
  | No-slot | 108 | −2.23% (s.e. 0.65) | −1.98% | 59% |
  | Capped while slots sat empty | 10 | −1.75% | +0.97% (TROW, MRNA, LYV and GM would have hit the target; SYK ×2, CMG and GNRC the stop) | 40% |

- **The entry signal itself was no better than random.** Across all 532 unique passers, the 20-day excess vs RSP was **−1.02%**; clustered
  by date it was −0.61% (s.e. 0.43, 103 days). Per closed trade, the excess vs RSP over the same holding period was **+0.08% (s.e. 0.93)**,
  hit rate 52%. No logged feature ranked the passers either. RSI level, RSI change, MACD change, ADX, volume ratio and 3- or 6-month relative
  strength all had |Spearman| ≤ 0.07 against the 20-day excess, and no tercile was consistently positive. This **confirms** the earlier
  "entry ≈ random" finding for this window. The entered group beat the skipped groups only because of alphabet luck.

## 6. Why it trailed SPY

Daily arithmetic attribution against SPY-TR, 07-09c→12-30c: −3.49 pp arithmetic, −3.84 pp compounded.

| Driver | pp | Evidence |

| **Cash and underexposure** | **−1.85** | Average cash 13.1%. It breaks down into three parts. **−0.53** came while 8/8 slots were "full" (the stubs below). −0.36 is the first-day alignment. **−0.96** came from empty slots: re-entry lag after exits, 47% cash after three stops on 11-19/11-22, and the regime block on 12-03..12-07 |
| Defensive ETFs | 0.00 | Bear mode never engaged |
| **Equal-weight vs cap-weight** (RSP − SPY on the invested sleeve) | **−2.14** | RSP made +8.0% against SPY +10.1% from the 07-12 open. H2-2021 was a mega-cap tape, and an 8-name equal-weight book is structurally on the wrong side of it |
| **Stock selection vs RSP** | **+2.37** on a price basis, **+3.03** on a total-return basis | Jul–Sep 20 −3.14, Sep 20–Dec +5.50. Per trade it is +0.08% ± 0.93, statistically nothing |
| Dividends not credited | **−0.66** | The result's `return_basis` is price_return, but the benchmark is total return. Measured $658 of dividends (AFL ×2, FE, ETR, VTR, GPC, NOC, AMAT, ELV). PBCT and ATVI are unpriced, so this is a lower bound |
| **Costs** | **−1.86** | $1,855 on 85 fills |
| Exits cutting winners | not in the NAV; see below | |

**Sizing bug that created idle cash.** It happens when an RSI or close-stop exit and an entry fall in the same session. The strategy credits the
exit proceeds (`strategy_swing.py:476-478`) and emits `buy_cash` of about $12.2k. The broker's buy gate, however, sees only pre-sale cash:
`Buy gate inputs for ELV: cash=$1631.25 … cash_to_use=$1631.25`. So ELV filled **4 of 32 shares** on 09-01 and DRI filled **14 of 87** on 10-22.
Each stub then held a slot and a sector for 5–7 weeks: ELV blocked healthcare names JNJ, BIIB, IDXX, UHS, DVA, DGX, RMD and SYK. About 11% of
equity sat idle from 09-01 to 11-26. The net P&L effect was about +$0.3k of missed gain: ELV would have added about +$0.98k, and DRI
would have lost about −$0.68k. That is luck. The mechanism itself costs money in any rising market.

**Did exits cut winners? Partly, and the stops cut them too.** Over the 20 sessions after the exit:

| Exit type | n (priced) | Average move | Excess vs RSP | Share higher |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Target | 15 | **+4.2%** | +3.9 pp | 10 of 15 (ANET +31.9%, DVN +16.4%, AFL +10.6%, MLM +7.8%; AMAT −8.6%, APA −4.8%) |
| Stop | 15 | **+4.9%** | +2.7 pp | 12 of 15 (DVN +19.6%, CSCO +12.7%, AVY +10.5%, DOW +9.6%) |
| RSI-70 | 4 | −3.3% | −3.0 pp | 1 of 4 |

- Both bracket legs sold into a drift that continued. The RSI-70 exit was the only well-timed exit.
- Redeployed trades averaged +1.2% gross. Holding the target-hit names 20 more sessions instead would have been worth about +0.5 pp
  (15 × 12.5% × 3 pp). The stops, a similar amount.

**Verdict.** The shortfall is structural, not bad luck:
- Costs, cash, missing dividends and the equal-weight tilt drag about −6.5 pp over 5.6 months.
- Selection added a noise-level +3 pp.

Gross of costs and dividends, the strategy beat RSP by about 1 pp but still trailed SPY by about 1.3 pp. This confirms the earlier
research's "trails SPY after costs" for this bull window. The drawdown claim is not tested here (§3).

## 7. Change hypotheses (evidence only; nothing implemented)

1. **Stop clipping entries that share a session with an exit.** Either size the buy against cash settled before it, or defer the entry one session.
   - *Observation:* ELV filled 4 of 32 shares and DRI 14 of 87. About 11% of equity sat idle for 12 weeks while the book logged 8/8 full. Cash
     drag while "full" was −0.53 pp, and the stubs also blocked their sectors.
   - *Expected effect:* +0.3–0.6 pp in a rising window, and exposure close to the intended 8 × 12.5%.
   - *Risk:* low. It is a mechanics fix with no parameter to tune, though bear windows gain a little exposure. The same pattern probably
     exists live: check `_consider`/`swing_live_budget` against pending-sale proceeds.
2. **Sweep idle cash into SPY (or RSP) while regime_ok, and sell it before each entry.**
   - *Observation:* 13.1% average cash cost −1.85 pp, and slot re-entry lag alone cost about −1 pp. The strategy's value, if any, is
     in the regime gate, and a sweep keeps it.
   - *Expected effect:* +1 to +1.5 pp in bull windows.
   - *Risks:* extra fills at 23 bps (46 swaps ≈ −0.1 to −0.2 pp). If the sweep is not gated by the regime, it adds beta exactly when the gate
     is meant to be in cash. It must be judged on W2 (2022) and W6 (2025 tariff shock).
3. **Widen or reshape the bracket: ATR-scaled stop, trailing or no fixed target, anchored on the fill. Keep the RSI-70 exit.**
   - *Observation:* stopped names rose +4.9% over the next 20 sessions (12 of 15 higher), and target-hit names rose +4.2% (10 of 15). The −6%
     stop sits inside ordinary 3-week noise. Payoff is only 1.24. Anchoring on the prior close gave DVN and VTR 7–8% stops and ADBE a stop above its fill.
     Turnover of about 11.5× a year pays about 4%/yr in costs.
   - *Expected effect:* fewer whipsaw round trips, lower cost, and more of the winner tail (ANET, DVN, AFL).
   - *Risk:* **regime-specific.** H2-2021 drifted up, so any "hold longer" rule looks good here. Larger per-trade losses in 2022 are likely. It needs every window.
4. **Cap new entries per session (for example 2–3) and stagger fills on mass-signal days.**
   - *Observation:* 50 passers on 07-12 filled 8 slots at once, and 5 were stopped within 18 sessions (NAV −4.4% vs SPY −2.4% in 5 sessions).
     Entries on sessions with more than 15 passers had a 20-day excess of −1.4% (n=20). Sessions with 15 or fewer had +3.1% (n=23). The
     date-level correlation of pass count with outcome is only −0.10.
   - *Expected effect:* less cohort risk and a smoother start.
   - *Risk:* **overfitting.** The evidence is essentially one or two clusters (07-12, 07-21). It may under-deploy in V-shaped recoveries.
5. **Stop letting list order pick the trades.** Rank the passers by a pre-registered key, or run seeded random orders and report the distribution.
   - *Observation:* the company-name alphabet decides which of about 10 passers enter. The entered names beat the capped and no-slot names by
     2–3 pp over 20 days on luck alone. The earlier research found a p5–p95 spread of +8.6% to +48.3% from order alone.
   - *Expected effect on mean return:* **none.** No logged feature (RSI, MACD, ADX, volume, 3- or 6-month relative strength) ranked the passers
     here, with |ρ| ≤ 0.07. The value is in stopping order luck from being read as signal in these window verdicts.
   - *Risk:* cost only, since this needs more backtests.
6. **Use point-in-time sectors and an earnings blackout.**
   - *Observation:* `SwingSectorMap` is yfinance labels as of 2026-09-25. It has 41 "unknown" rows for delisted names, and those escape the
     1-per-sector cap: PBCT (a bank) sat beside AFL, then BAC, and ATVI was unknown too. Two of 19 losing stops were earnings-day moves
     (CI 08-05 gap-through, CSCO 11-18).
   - *Expected effect:* correctness, plus about +0.2 pp.
   - *Risk:* low, apart from the data work (point-in-time GICS and earnings dates). An earnings rule also removes some gap-up winners.

**Do not change the regime gate on this evidence.** It cost about 0.7 pp here by blocking 12-03..12-07 at the Omicron low, and the SPY leg nearly
tripped at the 10-05 low. That is the gate's known bull-market lag, and its value (if any) is in bear windows this run does not contain.
