# W2-bear-2022: backtest 588533 (swing-paper, doc 204), 2022-01-03 to 2022-10-14

**Sources.** The uncapped log (`logs/588533.log`, 2,862 lines), every `BacktestSteps` row, `BacktestResults`, and the lane's own `SwingDailyBars`, membership and sector rows (all read-only). SPY and ETF total returns come from yfinance with auto_adjust. Scratch work is in `swing_bt/w2/`.
**Replay.** An independent replay of the exit rules on the lane's IEX bars reproduces all 31 round trips exactly: the same exit day, exit reason and fill price to within 0.01%. The counterfactuals below use that replay (`w2/sim.py`).

## 0. Accounting audit (priority check): clean books, two fidelity bugs

**The −15.31% is real.**
- **Mark-to-market.** `value = cash + Σ qty × price` holds to $0.00 on all 205 pv days. The largest gap is 0.000%. No held symbol is ever missing a price, and no day has negative cash.
- **Cash ledger.** Replaying the 62 fills reproduces every `cash_after`. The only gap is +$0.47 from seq 17 onward, which is the QQQ dividend credit (`dividends_credited` $0.4666). Σ round-trip P&L is −$15,310.82 against a NAV change of −$15,310.35.
- **Specific checks.**
  - Each of the 31 buy lots is sold exactly once, in full. No sell is larger than the position.
  - The 4 signal exits (ITW, AMD, XLP, XLU) cancelled their bracket legs, and no later leg fill exists.
  - All 32 buy-gate lines show `reserved=$0.00`. There is no `NEXT-OPEN EXPIRED` line, and unfilled is 0.
  - Fees are 0.3 bps per fill and spread is at most 22.85 bps. Both are charged on the fill's own notional.
- **Why 21% exposure still lost 15%.** The loss is per trade, not per unit of exposure. There were 27 losers averaging −$690 each: a stop of about −6% on a 12.5% slot costs about 0.75% of NAV. The 4 winners made +$2,925 in total. Bought notional came to 3.8× NAV.
- **Double-counted trades in metrics.json.** Every `BacktestSteps` row is stored twice under the same seq, plus one null seq-0 row. That gives 410 pv rows (205 × 2) and 124 trade rows (62 × 2). So `metrics.json` "trade_steps: 125" double-counts. The run made **62 fills, or 31 round trips.** The pv and trade duplicates are identical, so the averages are unaffected.

**Bug 1: the strategy's buying power and the engine's differ, and the engine drops the difference without a trace (2022-02-10).**
- The strategy credited AMD's exit to buying power: $12,355.98, valued at the 132.86 close (fix "F1"). It then emitted 4 entries: ACN, AME, ESS for 35 shares and MLM for 23 shares (decision seqs 22–25).
- The engine's `execute_signal` sizes buys against *settled* cash less the same-tick reservations. It does not count the pending AMD sale, because doc 204 does not set `backtest_credit_pending_sell_proceeds`. It also withholds 5% of the 02-08 sales (ITW, ESS, INFO), about $1,555.
- The arithmetic: 32,594.78 − 1,555 − 2 × 11,505.27 = $8,029, which buys floor(8,029 / (328.58 × 1.00228)) = **24 ESS shares** (fill seq 37, order_quantity 24). That left $0 for **MLM, which got no order, no fill, no refusal row and no log line**. `unfilled_order_count` stays 0, while the log still says "ENTER 23 sh".
- The P&L effect was +$780 of luck: MLM would have gap-stopped on 02-24 at −6.6% (−$597), and ESS's 11 missing shares avoided $183. The real damage is that the log and the fills diverge on any session where exits fund entries.

**Bug 2: the sector map is not point-in-time, so delisted names escape the 1-per-sector cap.**
- `SwingSectorMap` was built from yfinance as of 2026-09-25. It maps 41 names that were later delisted or acquired to `unknown`, including FRC, DRE, INFO and HOLX.
- `sector_conflict` exempts `unknown`, so the cap was bypassed 4 times:

| Name let in | Already held, same sector | Session |
|---|---|---|
| HOLX | REGN (healthcare) | 01-11 |
| FRC | MRSH (financials) | 01-11 |
| DRE | ESS (real estate) | 02-03 |
| INFO | ITW (industrials) | 02-03 |

- Those four lost −$2,973. The names that would have taken their slots lost about as much, so the net effect in this window is about 0. It is still survivorship bias, and the rule is not applied as written.
- XLP, XLU, XLV and SHY are also `unknown`, so the defensive ETFs are uncapped among themselves. GLD maps to `commodity`.
- A minor note: a CFG bar-chunk request timed out (2021-02-02..2022-02-02, log 18:30:00). CFG's fill and stop still reproduce exactly, so it had no effect.

## 3. Regime

**Session counts.** Of 198 sessions, **15 were ACTIVE** (01-03..01-19, plus single sessions on 02-03, 02-10 and 03-30). 31 were blocked but not yet in bear mode, and **152 were bear-mode sessions** (02-02; 02-25..03-29; 04-13..10-14).

**What did the blocking.** The SPY leg blocked all 183 blocked sessions. VIX was above 25 on 113 of them but never blocked on its own. SPY was below its plain SMA200 on 162 sessions. **The 3% buffer alone blocked 21 sessions**, 19 of them with VIX ≤ 25. In the replay, the entry signal on those 19 sessions averaged −3.4% per trade with a 23% win rate, so the buffer helped.

**SPY during each blocked stretch** (closes around the stretch; NAV over the same stretch):

| Blocked stretch | SPY | NAV | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01-20..02-02 (10 sessions) | +1.2% | −2.8% | SPY troughed at −4.5% on 01-27; the January book rode its stops |
| 02-04..02-09 | +2.5% | +0.1% | |

| 03-31..10-14 (137 sessions) | −21.4% | −3.7% | |

**The gate helped on level.** The book was 14% invested across bear mode, and 0% after 09-26.

**The gate hurt on timing, in three ways.**
1. **It was late.** It closed on 01-20, by which point SPY was already −4.9% from its 12-31 close. Five of the January stops (ALB, FRC, TT, FTNT, HOLX) hit while the gate still read ACTIVE. SPY's prior close was already below its SMA50 going into the 01-10, 01-11, 01-14, 01-18 and 01-19 sessions. The strategy entered on four of them; 01-14 had no free slot.
2. **It flickered.** Three single-session re-openings cleared the 1.03×SMA200 line by only 0.20%, 0.05% and 0.27%. They produced 11 fills worth −$5,089, with 10 losers. Each flicker also reset the bear counter, which switched bear mode off for about 30 sessions. That cost little, because only one ETF signal fired in that span (XLU on 02-10, −5.1% in the replay).
3. **It never acts on held names.** When it blocks, the book keeps riding its brackets. 18 stock positions were still open at the first blocked open after an ACTIVE session.

**Were entries made just before regime flips? Yes.** 14 of the 23 stock entries came within 2 sessions of the gate closing: 01-18, 01-19, 02-03, 02-10 and 03-30. They lost −$7,131, and 13 of the 14 lost.

**Did the defensive ETF book protect capital? No.**
- **It lost money.** Its 8 trades lost −$2,994: 6 hit the stop and 2 were RSI-70 winners.
- **Every defensive ETF fell in 2022.** Total returns over 01-03..10-14: XLP −9.2%, XLU −10.0%, XLV −10.5%, GLD −9.4%, SHY −4.8%, for an equal-weight −8.8%. From 04-13 to 10-14 the equal-weight basket lost −10.9% against SPY's −15.8%.
- **SHY never qualified.** It was above its SMA200 on 0 of the 152 bear-mode sessions.
- **The protection was cash.** Bear mode was 86% cash because the ETFs failed `close > SMA200` (the median session had 0 of 5 above it). Only 7 of the 152 bear-mode sessions had a signal at all.
- **Relative to SPY it did a little better.** Over the same holding windows, 6 of the 8 ETF trades beat SPY, worth +$1,434.

## 4. Funnel

The median ACTIVE session ran: universe 507 → not held 504 → has indicators 498 → **above SMA200 348 → RSI < 50 and rising 16** → MACD improving 8 → volume 3 → ADX 3 → **passed 3**.

**Which filter kills the most.** Summed over sessions, RSI < 50-and-rising removes **4,592 of the 5,042 name-sessions above SMA200 (91%)**. Above SMA200 removes 2,422 more, MACD removes 183 and volume removes 144. ADX > 15 is nearly inert (6), and `rr_ok` does nothing (9/6 is always ≥ 1.5).

**Pass counts.** 12 of the 15 ACTIVE sessions passed at least one name. 01-12 and 01-13 passed 26 and 29, the day before and in the middle of the slide.

**Full book.** **All 8 slots were full on 3 scan sessions (01-12, 01-13, 01-14)**, and on 6 pv days in total: 01-12..01-14 and 02-04..02-08 (the 02-03 cohort).

**Sector cap.** It blocked 77 entries (71 named in the log), and the slot limit blocked 15. **The blocked names did not outperform:**

| Group | Mean per trade | Wins |
|---|---|---|
| Sector-capped names, replayed with the exact exit rules | −5.41% | 4 of 71 (ORLY +10.1%, CNC +9.3%, STE +8.5%, CME +8.4%) |
| Slot-blocked names | −7.13% | 0 of 15 |
| Stock trades actually taken | −4.63% | |

Both caps saved money in this window.

**Is the entry better than random? No, it did worse.** I reproduced the signal from the lane's bars; the pass counts come within 0–4 of the logged ones on every session. With the same exits:

| Entry set (same 15 ACTIVE sessions, same exits) | n | Mean per trade | Win rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Every signal pass** | 127 | **−5.38%** | 6% |
| Every member above SMA200 | 5,098 | −3.80% | 22% |
| Every member | 7,560 | −3.82% | 20% |

In bear mode, the ETF signal averaged −3.70% against −2.33% for a random ETF entry. The 2026-09-24 finding that the entry is "about as good as random" holds here, and in this window the entry did worse than random. It buys the first bounce of a leg down.

## 6. Why it beat SPY (+8.96 pp)

The trade-by-trade attribution asks what each trade's notional would have done in SPY over the identical holding window (open to exit):

| Component | pp |
|---|---|
| **Cash and underexposure.** SPY −24.27% at 100% invested vs −12.86% for the actual exposure windows. | **+11.4** |
| Stock selection vs SPY, same windows (January −1.41, flips −2.46). Against equal-weight S&P members it is −5.49. | **−3.9** |
| Defensive ETFs vs SPY, same windows | **+1.4** |
| Costs (included in the selection lines; selection before costs is −0.9 pp) | −1.6 |
| Exits cutting winners (REGN about −1.2; AMD's and ITW's exits avoided −12.6% and −8.5% moves) | ≈ 0 |

**A caveat on the cash line.** Holding windows that end at a stop are biased toward down days for SPY, so the "timing" split overstates how much of the loss was market direction. The cleaner reference is the random-entry replay in section 4: a random S&P name bought on these sessions with these exits lost −3.8% per trade, and the taken stocks lost −4.6%.

**Net.** Cash is the whole story, as the earlier research concluded. Stock picks cost about 0.8 pp per trade against random in this window (−4.63% vs −3.80%). The defensive ETFs added nothing absolute.

## 7. Change hypotheses (implement nothing)

1. **Require confirmation before stock entries after a block, and don't reset bear mode on a single ACTIVE session.**
   - *Observation:* three one-session flips, each clearing the line by 0.05–0.27%, produced 11 fills worth −$5,089 (10 of 11 lost). They also switched bear mode off for about 30 sessions.
   - *Proposal:* require ≥ 3 consecutive ACTIVE sessions, or a clearance band above 1.03×.
   - *Expected effect:* about +5 pp here.
   - *Risk:* slower re-entry after V-shaped bottoms. Test on W3/W4, where the gate re-opens for real.
   - *Not the fix:* an SMA50 filter would have blocked 7 of the 15 ACTIVE sessions but not 01-12 or 01-13. On those days 26–29 names passed and averaged −6 to −7% in the replay, so January would likely have lost about as much.
2. **Exit the stock book at the next open when the gate closes.**
   - *Observation:* the 18 stock positions open at a blocked open would have lost −$2,644 instead of −$8,429, a gain of **+$5.8k**, even after giving up REGN and AMD (−$1.56k). With hypothesis 1 in place, only the January part remains: +$2.2k.
   - *Risk:* whipsaw around the 1.03 line, at about 46 bps per round trip. In bull chop, where W1 lived, it could churn.
3. **In bear mode, hold cash or T-bills instead of swing-trading XLP, XLU, XLV and GLD.**
   - *Observation:* all five ETFs fell 4.8–10.5% total return in the window. SHY never qualified. The ETF signal lost −3.7% per trade against −2.3% for random entries, and the sleeve lost −$2,994.
   - *Expected effect:* about +3 pp here.
   - *Risk:* this is very regime-specific. 2022 was a rate-shock bear in which bonds and defensives fell together, whereas GLD and SHY rallied in 2008 and 2020. Don't adopt it on one window.
4. **Anchor the −6%/+9% bracket on the fill price, or skip opens that gap more than ±1% from the prior close.**
   - *Observation:* the effective stop ranged from −3.3% to −8.0%. The gap-up entries ALB, ITW and GLD lost 6.5–7.5%, while the gap-down entries ESS and ACN were stopped out on noise.
   - *Expected effect:* a uniform loss per stop, worth a few hundred dollars here.
   - *Risk:* it departs from the verbatim port of the original strategy.
5. **Fix fidelity before trusting any window comparison.**
   - *Proposal:*
     - Either set `backtest_credit_pending_sell_proceeds` on doc 204's lane, or stop crediting exit proceeds in the strategy's buying power.
     - Log every ENTER the engine drops or resizes.
     - Give delisted names point-in-time sectors, or cap `unknown` as its own sector.
   - *Expected effect:* about P&L-neutral here.
   - *Risk:* none for the strategy, but it breaks byte-identity with earlier runs.
6. **Leave the entry thresholds, the sector cap and the slot count alone.**
   - *Observation:* the blocked names lost more than the taken ones (−5.4% and −7.1% against −4.6%), and the signal did worse than random.
   - *Implication:* tuning RSI and MACD is not the lever in a bear; when the gate lets entries through is.
   - *Risk:* a single window with 15 ACTIVE sessions. The sample is small and the entries within a session are correlated.
