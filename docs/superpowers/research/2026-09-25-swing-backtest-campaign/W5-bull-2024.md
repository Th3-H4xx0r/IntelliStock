**Verdict: which cause drove the loss?**
- **Mega-cap concentration explains over a third of the gap to SPY (−6.7 pp).** It is structural: SPY without its top 11 companies matched RSP. The
  strategy could reach neither NVDA-style leaders (a pullback entry rarely fires on them) nor tech (ROP held the slot all year). Under
  30 random tie-break orders the same rules returned −3.5% ± 3.4 (range −11.0% to +3.6%). **They never beat RSP and always trailed SPY by at least 9.9 pp.**
  The engine's −4.98% is a typical draw, not bad luck.
- **The absolute loss came from two stop clusters.** The April pullback cost −7.8% (−3.7 pp of it selection, mostly the 04-01 burst), and the
  July rotation plus the yen-carry spike cost −8.7% (−4.5 pp selection, then −1.9 pp of cash while the gate and the empty book sat out the
  rebound). **The yen-carry leg was the worst single episode, but the typical ordering lost only −4.1% there, against −7.6% on the replica's
  alphabetical path (engine −8.7%)**: that path held a particularly bad cohort (GOOGL, MRK, LLY, AIG, MRO, AVY).
- **Whipsaw stops:** the stop produced every loss, but removing it is worse (§7, H5). **Sector mix: small** (−0.9 pp). **VIX gate: neutral.**
- **December was equal weight's loss, not the strategy's:** −5.6% against RSP −6.5%.

**Against the earlier research:** entry ≈ random is **confirmed**; trailing SPY after costs is **confirmed** (−18.5 pp, and about −14.5 pp
gross of costs and dividends); the drawdown benefit from the regime filter is **not seen** (−9.43% against −8.41%, with 4 blocked sessions).

- "Unknown" sectors: MRO and K (K let CLX sit beside it); capping "unknown" leaves the replica unchanged. Late, flickering gate: not seen (§3).
- Bracket stops near lows, sells leaders early: **half true** (August lows yes; the 17 target exits then fell 2.9%). Alphabetical tie-break:

## 7. Change hypotheses (evidence only; nothing implemented)

1. **Run the swing book as a satellite beside a cap-weighted core (SPY or QQQ), or judge it against RSP, not SPY.**
   - *Observation:* SPY's lead over equal weight came entirely from about a dozen mega caps. The funnel passed them on 1–8 of 187 sessions, and the
     sector cap blocked most of those. The best of 30 orderings (+3.6%) still trailed RSP. The book was 89% invested, so W4's idle-cash sleeve
     would add at most about +2 pp here.
   - *Expected effect:* a 50/50 SPY core would have made about +4.3% here, cutting the gap from −18.5 pp to about −9.3 pp. It fixes the
     benchmark problem, not the signal.
   - *Risk:* the core is always long, so it gives back part of the gate's bear-market protection (W2). It is beta, not alpha.
2. **Drop the fixed +9% target; keep the −6% stop and the RSI-70 exit.**
   - *Observation:* per trade it barely matters (the 17 target trips make +9.57% instead of +8.98%). But fewer round trips (46 against 68 on
     the actual path) mean less cost and fewer re-entries into falling tapes.
   - *Replica:* **+3.64 pp paired, better in 25 of 30 orders** (mean −3.51% → +0.13%). W4 found +1.9 pp; two windows agree.
   - *Risk:* in a bear, winners give back to the stop. Test on W2's entries before anything else.
3. **Cap new entries at two a session.**
   - *Observation:* the 04-01 burst put 87% of equity into 7 names in one session, and every passer of that session lost (−4.8% replayed);
     that cohort's −$3,879 is most of the endpoint loss.
   - *Replica:* **+2.28 pp paired, better in 22 of 30** (April leg −6.95% against −8.40%). Combined with H2, +4.66 pp (21 of 30); 8 of 30 orders then beat RSP, and 1 beat SPY.
   - *Risk:* part of the gain is a backtest-start artifact, since a live book rarely has 7 free slots at once. W4 found +3.5 pp and W3 found the cap
     worse. It also slows re-entry after a V-bottom: here the 08-09 burst (5 entries) won.
4. **Free a sector slot from a stale holding.** For example, let a new same-sector signal replace a holding that is within ±3% of its fill
   after N sessions, rather than applying a blanket time stop.
   - *Observation:* ROP sat on the tech slot for 181 sessions at −3.3% and blocked 42 tech names that replay at +1.75% (60% won), including
     MSFT, AAPL and AVGO. CHD held staples for 155 sessions.
   - *Replica:* weak. A blanket 40-session time stop gains +0.47 pp (19 of 30). On the single path, 20 and 30 sessions do worse
     (−10.9% and −5.2%) and 60 better (+1.6%): non-monotonic, so this is noise.
   - *Risk:* the case rests on one name, and every rotation adds turnover at ~23 bps a fill. Only test the replacement rule, not a time stop.
5. **Do not widen, re-anchor or remove the −6% stop on this window's evidence.**
   - *Observation:* the stop produced all 35 losers, and the August cluster whipsawed. But over 30 orders: RSI-70 exit only −9.27% (worst trade
     −44%), stop −10% −3.90% (drawdown −11.3%), fill-anchored −6%/+9% −4.46%, 2×ATR stop with a 3×ATR target −4.03%, 2.5×ATR stop with no
     target −0.53% (no better than H2 alone, +0.13%). All sit within ordering noise of the actual −3.51% or below it.
   - *Implication:* the stop is not the lever. The entry has no edge for any exit rule to protect; W4 pointed the other way on a V-bottom.
   - *Risk:* this rests on one window in which equal-weight names had almost no drift for a pullback entry to work with.

## Data caveats
- Replays use the engine's IEX bars (fills can differ by a few bps); the ordering runs vary only the tie-break, not the market path.
- Mega-cap and SPY sector weights are approximate end-March 2024 values (MSFT 7.1, AAPL 5.6, NVDA 5.1, AMZN 3.7, META 2.4, GOOGL 2.0, GOOG 1.7,
  BRK.B 1.7, LLY 1.4, AVGO 1.3, JPM 1.3, TSLA 1.0%) with buy-and-hold drift; treat the +10.6 pp as ±1 pp.
- Earnings attributions (GOOGL 07-23 and 10-29, MRK 07-30) are public record, not verified in-data (yfinance's calendar needs `lxml`, not
  installed). yfinance lacks EA, K and MRO, so the dividend figure is a lower bound.
